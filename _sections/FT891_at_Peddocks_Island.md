# A New Radio at Peddocks Island

This past weekend, September 19 and 20, 2026, I camped on Peddocks Island with my son's Scout troop,
Troop 119, Lexington. This is our fourth year making the trip, though only my third time activating the
park. Our first year, 2023, remnants of a hurricane were moving up the coast, so I left my HF gear home
and played around with APRS instead
([Camping on Peddocks Island in Boston Harbor]({% link _sections/Peddocks_Island.md %})). Since then
I've activated every year
([2025]({% link _sections/Back_to_Peddocks_Island.md %}), [2024]({% link _sections/POTA_Paddocks_Island.md %})),
and this time I brought along my new Yaesu FT-891 for one of its first outings.

![My Yaesu FT-891 and quarter-wave vertical set up on a picnic table at Peddocks Island, Fort Andrews in the background](PXL_20260919_175937642.jpg)

## Breaking in the FT-891

I set up my quarter-wave vertical and put the FT-891 on the air. It did great. I
worked both SSB and CW on 20 meters all afternoon.

![Chris Farnham (W1YTQ) logging a contact by hand next to the FT-891](FullSizeRender.jpg)

> 10 meters was wide open. I worked two stations in Brazil, [PY2TN](https://www.qrz.com/db/PY2TN) in
> Birigui and [PU1JSV](https://www.qrz.com/db/PU1JSV) in Rio de Janeiro, on SSB.

I was lucky to find 10 meters open with some SSB activity to South America, a real treat. Between
the two bands I also picked up Norway, Spain, France, Italy and Canada, all with the FT-891 and that
same quarter-wave whip. Every single contact I made on our first day was with the Yaesu, 31 of them by the
time the sun went down.

![Chris Farnham (W1YTQ) operating the FT-891 on the green in front of the Peddocks Island chapel](FullSizeRender_2.jpg)

## A late night with my KH1

Once the Scouts had turned in, I walked down to the beach with my beloved [KH1]({% link _sections/New_KH1_Elecraft_HF_Radio.md %}) and operated by
lantern-light. I love this little radio.

![My KH1 and its whip antenna lit by lantern on a picnic table, with the Boston Harbor shoreline lights across the dark water](PXL_20260920_010346474.jpg)

I got on 40 meters just after midnight UTC and worked stations up and down the East Coast before calling
it a night.

## Finishing the activation the next morning

I was back down at the water's edge in the morning to finish up. 20 meters was in great shape and I
had a steady run of CW contacts, including one station, [WC1D](https://www.qrz.com/db/WC1D) in
Pepperell, MA, that I'd also worked the day before on the FT-891.

![My KH1 set up on a picnic table by the beach, looking out at the pier and the water](PXL_20260920_135216149.jpg)

![Selfie of Chris Farnham (W1YTQ) operating the KH1 with the harbor behind him](PXL_20260920_143418000.jpg)

Every contact I made on the second day was with the KH1. Between the two radios I made 45 contacts across two
days, on 10, 20 and 40 meters, and activated US-2421 with plenty to spare.

![Map of all the contacts made over the weekend, with lines reaching across the US and into Europe and South America](450c6afb-33e5-455c-9905-929ab866d937.png)

We crossed back across Boston Harbor with the skyline behind us, another good weekend of camping and
radio in the books.

![View of the Boston skyline from the boat on the ride home](PXL_20260920_162540570.jpg)

## Going forward

I'm really happy with how the FT-891 has performed in these early outings. Unlike my KH1 and my [Xiegu G90]({% link _sections/First_Contact.md %}), it doesn't
have a built-in antenna matching unit, so my resonant quarter-wave whip is my best field antenna for it
right now. If I want to pair it with my [Rybakov]({% link _sections/Rybakov_Antenna.md %}) or random
wire antenna, my other go-to field antennas, I'll need to pick up an external tuner like an LDG Z-100.

I plan to keep pairing the FT-891 with the quarter-wave vertical for future Scout trips, and save the
KH1 for quiet moments.
